CHORUS
PRODUCT VISION · NEAR HACKATHON 2026
Many people. One purpose. Intelligent execution.
Chorus is a collaborative financial and execution platform that brings people, businesses, money and AI agents together to accomplish things that would otherwise require multiple people, services, payments and disconnected tools.
People create a goal. Others contribute. Businesses allocate budgets. AI agents help plan and execute the work. Payments move across supported blockchain networks, and every action is accounted for.
Chorus makes it possible to move from “We should do this” to “It’s done.”
Brand promise: Bring people together. Make things happen.
Product category: Collaborative finance and autonomous execution.
Built on: NEAR Protocol.
Initial market: Creators, digital teams, communities and businesses.
Long-term ambition: Become the infrastructure through which people and AI agents coordinate resources, purchase services and execute shared objectives.
1. The problem Chorus solves
Today, getting something done with multiple people is unnecessarily fragmented.
Imagine a group of creators wants to launch a campaign.
They need to:
Agree on the objective.
Decide how much money is needed.
Collect contributions from different people.
Find suitable service providers.
Pay for research, design, video and distribution.
Coordinate who is responsible for what.
Track expenditure.
Deliver the finished campaign.
These activities happen across WhatsApp, bank transfers, spreadsheets, wallets, marketplaces, payment links and separate AI tools.
Businesses experience the same fragmentation, only at a larger scale.
Even when an AI agent can perform work, it often lacks a reliable way to receive an authorized budget, purchase services, coordinate with other agents and provide an accountable record of expenditure.
Chorus connects these disconnected activities into one coordinated experience.
The opportunity is not simply to make collecting money easier. It is to make shared resources useful.

3. How Chorus works
Chorus is organized around a central concept: The Project.
A project is a shared space where an objective, participants, funding, tasks, services, agents and expenditure come together.
It could be a family holiday, a creator campaign, a community event or a company’s marketing operation.
The core user journey
Create a project
 Describe what you want to accomplish, set a target budget and define the expected outcome.
Bring people together
 Invite contributors, team members, clients or business partners using a shareable link.
Fund the objective
 Participants contribute through supported payment routes. Chorus tracks each contribution against the project budget.
Plan the execution
 An AI agent converts the objective into tasks, identifies services and prepares a proposed spending plan.
Approve and execute
 Authorized people approve purchases or delegate execution within predefined policies. Agents pay compatible providers.
Track the outcome
 Chorus records transactions, task progress, service delivery, remaining funds and project results.
The user sees one experience, even though the underlying operations may involve several networks, payment protocols and external services.

5. The AI layer: Chorus Intelligence
AI must be central to the product, not a chatbot bolted onto the dashboard.
Chorus Intelligence turns a human-defined objective into an executable plan.
Example
A business creates this request:
We have $1,000. Research our competitors, prepare a marketing strategy, create five campaign assets and prepare a launch package.
Chorus Intelligence:
Understands the objective.
Breaks it into individual tasks.
Estimates costs and identifies required services.
Searches compatible service providers.
Compares offers and prepares a proposed execution plan.
Requests approval for spending.
Executes approved purchases through payment-compatible agents.
Tracks the work and reports the outcome.

Human control is fundamental
Chorus must have a policy engine.
A project owner can configure:
Maximum spending per transaction.
Total authorized budget.
Approved service categories.
Approved providers.
Human approval thresholds.
Agent permissions.
Expiry and revocation of permissions.
For example:
Under $10: automatic execution if the provider and service are approved.
$10–$100: approval from a project administrator.
Above $100: explicit owner approval.
These are example policies, not hardcoded financial rules.
An agent should operate with scoped, revocable authority rather than unrestricted access to a wallet. This is especially important when dealing with irreversible blockchain transactions.

Important financial architecture decision
There are three different concepts that must remain separate:
Contribution ledger: Who contributed, how much, and to which project.
Settlement: What actually happened to the funds onchain.
Spending authorization: What the agent or project owner is allowed to spend.
A ledger entry must never be treated as proof of payment.
For the hackathon, I would first validate the complete path from a NEAR Intents quote to the intended recipient, including the supported recipient account types and asset routes. Then decide whether funds should be held in a dedicated contract, a controlled account, or another supported settlement arrangement.
The MPP documentation and NEAR’s agentic payment guidance should determine the actual payment adapter implementation. NEAR
We should also test failed, expired, underpaid and duplicated payment events, along with spending authorization and refunds.

16. The final vision
Chorus starts with a simple human behavior: people coming together to accomplish something.
It evolves into a platform where those people can pool resources, organize work, purchase services and delegate execution to intelligent agents.
Businesses use it to manage procurement and operational budgets. Creators use it to produce work together. Communities use it to coordinate resources. Developers use its infrastructure to connect applications and agents.
Over time, Chorus becomes more than an application.
It becomes an open network connecting human intentions, shared resources, intelligent agents and service providers.
The ambition is to make coordinated execution as easy as sending someone a link.

My recommendation before we start coding
There is one critical decision I would lock down first: the first Chorus demonstration must be a complete, verifiable workflow, not a collection of attractive screens.
I would make the first build prove three things:
People can contribute toward a real project using supported NEAR payment routes.
An AI agent can execute an approved purchase through MPP.
